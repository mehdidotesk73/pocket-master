# Future

A **forecast** shows where some of your money is heading. It starts from your **latest record** and adds up its **projections**: the things you expect to come in or go out.

## The chart

- **Bars** are drawn on each day something happens, one day wide, in each projection's colour: a weekly ($50) is a ($50) bar every week, a monthly +$100 a +$100 bar on its day. Projections only share a bar when they fall on the same day. Money in stacks above zero, money out below.
- The **line** is the forecast: your starting balance plus everything so far. It's a staircase: flat while nothing happens, and a straight jump up or down on each day something does. A daily projection makes small steps every day.
- On the left, the **green line** is your past records.
- Tap or hover a bar to see that day's amounts and the forecast by then.

With no records yet, a forecast starts from $0 today, so you can set up projections before your first record.

The heading tells you the main finding. If the forecast goes below zero, it says when.

## Forecasts

Pick one from **Forecast** at the top. You can keep several, for example:

- **Everything**: all lines of your latest record (net equity), with pay, rent and spending.
- **Checking**: just checking (and maybe your cards), to see how checking swings during the month.

**Edit** changes the forecast's name, which lines of your latest record it starts from, and how many days ahead it runs. **Duplicate** and **Delete** are there too. **New** makes a new forecast and opens it for editing. Both editors sit at the bottom of the screen, so you can watch the chart change as you go. Nothing is kept until you tap **Save** or **Create**.

## Projections

A projection is an amount you expect. Usually you type it in: look at the **Past** tab (Timeseries, Averages, Rates), decide what you expect, and enter it here. Or let Past work it out for you (see **Linked to Past** below).

Tap **Add** to make one, or tap a projection to change it. To change the order, drag a projection by its grip (⋮⋮) on the left, or focus the grip and use the arrow keys. The order is saved, and the chart's colours follow it. Next to the name, pick the type:

- **Recurring**: an amount that repeats **every N days, weeks, months or years**, starting on a date you pick. For months and years, the start date's day is used each time; days past a month's end land on its last day. For example: pay +$3,200 every month on the 2nd, rent ($2,030) every month on the 1st, card spending ($50) every day.
- **One-time**: an amount on one date, e.g. a trip ($800) on Mar 3.

Type and Unit are wheels: they look like a field until you touch one, then it opens into a small drum. Swipe it, or tap the option you want.

At the bottom of the editor, the projection is **read back as one sentence**, e.g. *Every month on the 1st: ($2,030.00).* Tap it to see what it adds up to by the end of the forecast. A one-time date outside the forecast shows an amber "to check" note.

Amounts are **+** (coming in) or **−** (going out): tap the sign to switch. Owed amounts show in parentheses.

## Linked to Past

On the Past tab, **Project this** (under Averages and Rates) makes a Recurring projection whose amount comes from your records instead of being typed:

- **Average**: a line's average over the records you pick, e.g. Capital One's average balance over the past 90 days, every month.
- **Daily change**: how much a line moves per day on average over the records you pick: every day, or only the **rising** or **falling** days. For a card, *falling days* is your spending with payments left out. Repeating every week or month multiplies it by the days in between (7, or 30.44).

Its card says *Recurring · linked to Past* and, in one line, what it's measured from, e.g. *Amex's average daily change, falling days only, over the past 90 days*. The amount is **worked out again whenever your records change**, so it follows your latest spending.

The amount is locked. You can still change the name, how often it repeats and when it starts. To measure something else, **Remove** it and make a new one from Past.

This is a guide, not a promise. It's only as good as the projections you've entered.
